# Tennitus Product Pitch Brief

Use this brief with Lovable, Claude, or another AI design/writing tool to create a product pitch, landing page, pitch deck, or founder narrative for Tennitus.

## Pasteable Prompt

You are a senior product strategist, health-tech copywriter, and pitch deck editor. Create a clear, commercially credible product pitch for Tennitus.

Tennitus is a local-first iOS companion for people with tinnitus and sound sensitivity who want to understand their symptom patterns, manage spike episodes, and prepare clearer information for a GP, audiologist, ENT, or private hearing clinic.

The pitch must position Tennitus as a tracking, self-management, and appointment-preparation product. It must not position Tennitus as a cure, diagnosis tool, medical device, AI audiologist, CBT replacement, or clinically proven treatment.

## Core Problem

People with tinnitus often struggle to explain what is happening:

- Symptoms fluctuate across days, sleep, stress, noise exposure, headphones, work, travel, and specific sound environments.
- Spikes are hard to remember accurately after they pass.
- Audiology or GP appointments can become vague because the patient cannot easily show patterns, triggers, severity changes, or sound discomfort history.
- Existing apps often focus on masking, relaxation, or broad wellness. Many do not turn messy symptom history into a concise clinician-friendly report.

The product problem:

People need a low-burden way to capture tinnitus and sound-sensitivity patterns, then turn that data into something useful for personal reflection and healthcare conversations.

## One-Sentence Positioning

Tennitus helps people track tinnitus spikes, understand likely symptom associations, and generate appointment-ready reports for audiologists and GPs.

## Short Pitch

Tennitus is a practical tinnitus companion for people who want clarity before care. It combines fast daily check-ins, spike logs, trigger tracking, basic comfort sounds, audiogram context, sound discomfort notes, and clinician-ready PDF reports. Instead of promising a cure, Tennitus helps users collect better information, notice patterns, and prepare for more productive conversations with healthcare professionals.

## Target Users

Primary users:

- Adults with subjective tinnitus whose symptoms fluctuate.
- People who want to understand what may make their tinnitus worse.
- People preparing for a GP, audiology, ENT, or private tinnitus clinic appointment.

Secondary users:

- People with sound sensitivity, listening fatigue, hyperacusis-like symptoms, or discomfort from specific real-world sounds.
- Audiologists and tinnitus clinics that may benefit from structured pre-appointment patient history.

## Product Jobs

The pitch should show that Tennitus helps users:

- Capture tinnitus spikes when they happen instead of relying on memory.
- Track likely associations across sleep, stress, noise, headphone use, mood, and context.
- Add audiogram details so hearing-profile context can be included in the report.
- Match perceived tinnitus pitch and compare it cautiously with audiogram frequency regions.
- Record or describe bothersome real-world sounds and summarize sound discomfort patterns.
- Generate a concise report that can be read by a clinician in a few minutes.
- Get comfort support from simple masking sounds without medical treatment claims.

## MVP Feature Set

Include these features in the pitch:

- Tinnitus profile onboarding
- Daily check-ins
- Spike logging
- Trigger tracking
- Weekly deterministic insights
- Audiologist-ready PDF report
- Basic masking sounds
- Audiogram entry or report attachment
- Tinnitus pitch match
- Optional sound discomfort mapping
- Local-first storage, export, and delete controls

Avoid making AI central to the MVP. AI can be presented as a future optional assistant for summarizing already-structured data, not as the core product.

## Differentiation

The pitch should avoid saying Tennitus is just another tinnitus diary. The defensible wedge is:

Track for 14 days and generate an appointment-ready tinnitus report.

Key differentiators:

- Clinician-first reporting rather than generic wellness tracking.
- Low-burden check-ins that avoid making users obsess over symptoms.
- Data-quality-aware insights that show associations, not causes.
- Audiogram and tinnitus pitch context included as discussion material.
- Sound discomfort mapping for users who want to explain which sounds bother them and why.
- Local-first privacy stance for health-adjacent data.

## Evidence-Aware Claims

Allowed claims:

- Tinnitus is common and can fluctuate.
- Stress, sleep, noise exposure, and sound environments can be relevant context for many users.
- Repeated lightweight tracking can capture patterns that are hard to remember later.
- Audiograms can provide useful hearing-profile context.
- Research suggests tinnitus pitch can sometimes overlap with hearing-loss frequency regions, but this relationship is mixed and should not be treated as diagnosis.
- Structured reports can help users prepare for clinical conversations.

Avoid these claims:

- Cures tinnitus
- Treats tinnitus
- Prevents hearing loss
- Diagnoses tinnitus causes
- Replaces an audiologist
- Provides medical-grade sound therapy
- Clinically proven relief
- AI-powered diagnosis
- CBT or mental health therapy

Free tier:

- Onboarding
- Daily check-ins
- Spike logs
- Trigger tracking
- Seven-day history
- Basic sounds

Paid first test:

- One-off appointment-ready PDF report, suggested price GBP 7.99 to GBP 14.99.

Possible premium tier:

- Unlimited history
- Extended insights
- Trigger association charts
- CSV export
- Advanced report history
- Advanced sound favourites
- Audiogram trend tracking

Possible future B2B:

- Clinic-facing pre-appointment intake workflow.
- Structured patient history for audiologists or tinnitus clinics.

The strongest initial monetisation message:

Free tracking. Pay only when you want a polished report for your appointment.

## Pitch Tone

Use a tone that is:

- Calm
- Credible
- Practical
- Clinician-friendly
- Clear enough for consumers
- Commercially sharp without sounding exploitative

Avoid:

- Wellness hype
- Fear-based copy
- Over-medical language
- Generic AI startup language
- Claims that sound regulated or clinical without evidence

## Recommended Pitch Structure

Create a pitch using this structure:

1. Title
   - Tennitus
   - "Turn tinnitus history into a clearer healthcare conversation."

2. Problem
   - Tinnitus is personal, fluctuating, and hard to explain.
   - Patients often arrive at appointments with scattered memory instead of structured history.

3. Insight
   - The immediate unmet need is not another promise of relief.
   - The immediate unmet need is better symptom capture and appointment preparation.

4. Solution
   - Tennitus provides fast tracking, spike logs, trigger context, audiogram context, sound notes, and clinician-ready reports.

5. Product
   - Show the daily check-in, spike log, trends, audiogram context, sound discomfort mapping, and report export.

6. Differentiator
   - Clinician-first reporting, local-first privacy, low-burden tracking, and cautious evidence-aware analysis.

7. Business Model
   - Free tracking plus paid PDF reports.
   - Premium history and insights later.
   - Clinic workflow later if validated.

8. Go-To-Market
   - Start with tinnitus communities, hearing health content, audiology clinics, private hearing providers, GP-prep content, SEO around "what to tell audiologist about tinnitus", and app store search.

9. Validation Plan
   - Interview 10 tinnitus users.
   - Interview 5 audiologists.
   - Test whether users will pay for a report after 14 days.
   - Ask clinicians whether the report saves time or improves patient recall.

10. Closing
   - Tennitus does not promise to cure tinnitus.
   - It helps people understand, organize, and communicate their experience better.

## Founder Narrative

If creating founder copy, frame the founder as:

An applied AI and audio/product engineer building a practical health-adjacent tool that combines audio understanding, local-first product design, structured reporting, and cautious AI use.

Do not overclaim clinical expertise. Emphasize product, audio, AI systems, privacy, and practical workflow design.

## Final Instruction

Create the pitch in a way that would make a tinnitus user think "this could help me explain what is happening" and make an audiologist think "this could make the appointment more productive."
